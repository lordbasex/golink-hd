# go-link HD in 3D

go-link HD draws 3D worlds as well as 2D ones: low-poly scenes rendered in software with integers only, so a 3D game keeps the engine's promises (the same pixels and sound on every computer and in WebAssembly, save states, no dependencies, no graphics card). This document is the reference for the 3D part (`src/3d/`) and a step-by-step guide to make a 3D game with it.

The first 3D scene is the proof of concept `golinkhd_load_demo(e, 2)` (`tools/hdrun LIB --demo 3d`): a lived-in flat on the fifth floor of a city block, seen from above without its ceiling like a scale model.

## Rules

- **One engine, 2D and 3D.** The 3D renderer is a separate part (`src/3d/`, its own header `r3d.h`). The 2D engine never calls it; only 3D games do. Sound, controllers, save states, packages, text and the API are shared.
- **Old games stay the same.** A 2D game is drawn exactly as before: the 3D code only runs when a 3D game is loaded. Every change to the 3D part is checked against ANTÍDOTO's frames and sound (see [Checking compatibility](#checking-compatibility)).
- **Integers only.** Positions, light and colors are integers or fixed point, never `float`, as in the rest of the engine (`src/fixed.h`). The textures and the baked light are made with integer noise and integer rays, so they are identical everywhere.
- **The same picture on any number of cores.** Drawing runs on up to 4 cores (`hd_rows`, `src/par.c`), but every core draws every triangle in the same order and the world is split and joined back in order, so the picture never depends on the core count.
- **It can be left out.** `make NO_3D=1` builds the engine without the 3D part (a smaller WebAssembly, an old Raspberry Pi); demo 2 is then the platformer.

## Units

| Thing | Unit |
|---|---|
| Positions, sizes | 16.16 meters: `FX_ONE` is one meter, `FX_FRAC(n, 100)` is n centimeters |
| Axes | x east, y up, z north; a yaw of 0 looks toward +z |
| Angles | the engine's: 4096 a turn (`hd_sin`, `hd_cos` give 16384 for 1.0) |
| Light, strengths, exposure | 256 is 1.0 |
| Colors | `0xRRGGBB`; textures `0xAARRGGBB` |

## How a frame is made

1. **Building, once.** The game adds pieces (boxes, cylinders, balls, quads) with a material and a place. Each becomes triangles in world space. Faces bigger than the detail cell (`r3d_detail`, 40 cm in the demo) are cut into cells so the baked light can show shadows on them; faces that look down are not cut. Texture coordinates follow the piece's own axes (a rug's pattern turns with the rug) or stretch a picture once over a face.

   Round pieces keep a normal at each corner, so their light changes smoothly across them and they look round, not faceted:

   - cylinders of 6 sides or more (lamp shades, pots, stools);
   - balls (heads, hands, a beanbag).

   With `r3d_bevel` set, boxes thick enough get their edges cut at 45 degrees, each cut lit from one face's normal to the other's: furniture's edges catch the light like real ones.
2. **Baking, once.** `r3d_bake` fills a grid of 5 cm cubes (voxels) with the solid pieces (glass and thin things can be left out with `r3d_occlude(0)`), then walks rays through that grid:
   - five toward the sun, slightly apart: a soft shadow edge;
   - up to 26 around the face for the sky: corners, the floor under a table and the inside of a shelf get less (ambient occlusion);
   - one toward each lamp within its reach.
   Big faces (floors, walls, table tops: at least 15 cm on each side, not looking down, not glowing) get a **light map**: a small picture of their own with a texel every 5 cm that keeps how much sun and how much sky arrive there, softened once (3 × 3) to take the rays' grain away. Their corners keep the rest: the sun's light without shadows, the sky without its corners and the lamps. Smaller faces keep everything in their corners. So shadows are as sharp as the grid, not as the triangles. The grid only covers the box given in `r3d_light_cfg` (`lo`, `hi`): the flat itself; the city around is lit without shadows. The demo's flat bakes in about a second, on 4 cores.
3. **Drawing, every frame** (`r3d_draw`):
   1. the sky (a gradient) and an empty depth buffer;
   2. the world in runs of 64 triangles: a run whose bounds are out of view is skipped whole; the others are drawn nearest first (what is hidden behind them then fails the depth test before it costs anything), split between 4 cores and joined back in order;
   3. each triangle turned toward the eye is clipped to the view (in front of the near plane, inside the screen's edges) in camera space and projected with 4 bits of subpixel precision;
   4. its planes are set up (1/z, light, texture), each triangle on its own, on 4 cores;
   5. it is filled row by row: where each row enters and leaves the triangle is worked out at once, then each pixel tests the depth buffer, reads the texture (between its four nearest texels when it is seen bigger than its texels, so close-ups are not blocky), reads the light map between its four nearest texels (sky × the corners' light + sun × the face's sun light), multiplies it by the color, and turns the result into the screen's color through a filmic curve (ACES-like, worked out in integers);
   6. glass is drawn after everything else, over it, without depth: what is behind shows through, tinted;
   7. the edges are softened (a pixel that differs a lot from its neighbours takes some of them);
   8. with `bloom`, what gives light (glowing pieces: lamps, screens, the TV, a neon) spills softly around it. Each glowing pixel's light above white is kept in its own color, summed into a quarter-size copy, blurred twice and added back. A row is cleared only when something glows on it, and a pixel counts only while nothing nearer covers it, so the result never depends on earlier frames;
   9. with `vignette`, the corners get a little darker. This and the bloom are done in the same pass as the softening, band by band.
4. **What moves** (a character, a cat, a door) is an instance: a mesh drawn at a position and a turn (a yaw, or a whole turn with `rot`: `r3d_rotation(yaw, pitch, roll)`), lit by the sun and the sky as it goes (without shadows), after the world. That light depends only on the normal, so it is worked out once for 128 × 128 normals and looked up for each corner. A soft round shadow under it is a decal (`r3d_decal`): a quad that darkens what is under it.
5. **People** (`src/3d/actor3d.h`): a body of 11 parts (hips, torso, head with a face, hair and glasses, upper arms, forearms with hands, thighs, shins with shoes), each a mesh with its joint at the origin, built once with a look (skin, hair, clothes, shoes, glasses colors). `actor3d_place` poses it every frame into instances by turning each part around its joint: standing, walking (legs and arms swinging against each other, a small bob), sitting, typing (hands moving), waving, the head turned and tilted. A positive pitch swings a limb's end forward, toward the face.

## The API (`src/3d/r3d.h`)

### Textures

| Call | What |
|---|---|
| `r3d_tex_add(wlog, hlog, px)` | A picture of 2^wlog × 2^hlog pixels kept by the caller; returns its id. It repeats across faces. |
| `r3d_tex_pixels(id)` | Its pixels, to change them while playing (a screen that shows code). |
| `tex3d_build()` | Makes the built-in textures (`TEX3D_*`) once and returns their ids. |
| `tex3d_poster(kind, seed)` | A picture for a poster or a frame: a sun over hills, stripes, a terminal, circles. |

The built-in textures: parquet, long boards, tiles, patterned cement tiles, wall tiles, plaster, fabric, knit, two rugs, grass, concrete, brick, a city facade, wood grain, marble, brushed metal, leaves, cork and a soft round shadow. Plaster, fabric, knit, leaves and metal are grey on purpose: the piece's color tints them.

### Building

| Call | What |
|---|---|
| `r3d_clear()` | Forgets every triangle, solid and lamp. |
| `r3d_place(x, y, z, yaw)` | Where the next pieces go. Pieces are given in the place's own axes. |
| `r3d_begin()`, `r3d_end()` | The triangles added in between are a mesh (`r3d_mesh`): the world, or something that moves. |
| `r3d_material(tex, texels_per_m)` | The next pieces' texture: laid `texels_per_m` texels a meter, or stretched once over each face with 0. -1 is none. |
| `r3d_glow(strength)` | The next pieces glow by themselves (screens, bulbs, glass); 0 is off. |
| `r3d_occlude(on)` | Whether the next pieces stop light. |
| `r3d_decal(on)` | The next pieces darken what is under them (shadows, stains); their texture's alpha is how much. |
| `r3d_glass(cover)` | The next pieces are see-through glass (1..255: how much they cover), drawn after everything else. Use it with `r3d_occlude(0)` so light goes through. |
| `r3d_detail(cell)` | Faces bigger than `cell` are cut into cells for the baked light; 0 never. |
| `tex3d_terminal(id, seed, scroll, on)` | Redraws a 256-texel poster as a working terminal (code `scroll` pixels up, a blinking cursor) or asleep. A game calls it while drawing, from its state, for screens that change. |
| `r3d_bevel(size)` | Boxes built from now on get their edges cut this much (the demo uses 2 cm for furniture and people; 0 for walls and floors), where they are at least four times as thick. |
| `r3d_box(x0, y0, z0, x1, y1, z1, color, flags)` | A box; `R3D_SKIP_*` leave faces out, `R3D_PANE` keeps only the faces toward -z and +z (a picture, a screen), `R3D_TOP_ONLY` only the top (a floor, a rug), `R3D_INSIDE` turns its faces inward. |
| `r3d_cylinder(x, y, z, r, r_top, h, sides, color)` | A cylinder standing on (x, y, z); `r_top` 0 is a cone. |
| `r3d_ball(x, y, z, rx, ry, rz, rings, color)` | A low-poly ball. |
| `r3d_quad(a, b, c, d, color)` | A flat quad, one side. |
| `r3d_light(x, y, z, radius, color, strength)` | A lamp for the bake. |
| `r3d_bake(&cfg)` | Lights everything built so far (`r3d_light_cfg`: the sun's direction, color, strength and softness; the sky's and the ground's light; how far a corner looks for walls; the grid's box). |
| `r3d_solid_at(p)`, `r3d_reach(from, to)` | After the bake, the grid's solids for a game's rules: whether a point is inside one, and how far a straight line goes before one (0..65536 of its length). `play3d` builds on them. |

### Drawing

| Call | What |
|---|---|
| `r3d_draw(surface, &view, &scene, instances, count)` | The world and the instances seen from the view. `r3d_view`: the eye, yaw, pitch, field of view, near plane, the sky's two colors, exposure, `smooth` (softened edges), `bloom` and `vignette` (0..256; the demo and the game use 200 and 110) and the light for what moves. |
| `r3d_project(surface, &view, point, &x, &y)` | Where a point of the world lands on screen (a label over a lamp, a prompt over a door). |
| `r3d_triangles()`, `r3d_drawn()` | How many triangles were built, and how many the last frame drew. |
| `r3d_rotation(yaw, pitch, roll, m)`, `r3d_rot_mul`, `r3d_rot_apply` | Turns as 3 × 3 matrices (2.14), for instances that tilt (an arm, a door, a falling mug). |

### People (`src/3d/actor3d.h`)

| Call | What |
|---|---|
| `actor3d_build(&kit, &look, fabric_tex)` | Builds a person's parts once (after the bake). `kit.shadow` may hold a soft round decal drawn under the feet. |
| `actor3d_place(&kit, &pose, out)` | The pose's instances (up to `ACT_INSTANCES`) into `out`; returns how many. `actor3d_pose`: where the feet are, the floor for the shadow, the yaw (2048 faces +z), `walk` (0..16384) and `phase` (0..4095), `sit`, `type`, `wave`, `look` and `nod`; `hold` (the hands in front of the chest), `crouch` (the knees bent low) and `lie` (on the back, the whole body turned around the hips; `at.y` is what it lies on, and it has no floor shadow). |

People can hold things: a pose's `prop` (a mesh built around its grip, front toward -z) goes between both hands with `hold`, in front of the belly with `strum` (a guitar: one hand along the neck, the other strumming), or in the right one; `prop_pitch` and `prop_roll` turn it. `room3d_prop` has the flat's: a mug, a pan, a can, a bowl, a controller, a guitar, a watering can, a parcel, headphones, and the shower's water (moved down every frame).

### Pets (`src/3d/pet3d.h`)

| Call | What it does |
|---|---|
| `pet3d_build(&kit, &look)` | Builds a cat's parts once (after the bake); `look` holds the fur, the stripes, the white chest and paws, the eyes and the nose. `kit.shadow` may hold a decal. |
| `pet3d_place(&kit, &pose, out)` | The pose's instances (up to `PET_INSTANCES`) into `out`. `pet3d_pose`: the paws (`at`, with `at.y` over the floor when it is on a bed or a desk), the floor for the shadow, the yaw (as people), `walk` and `phase`, `sit`, `lie` and `look`. |

### Models made by artists (`src/3d/model3d.h`)

A package can bring models (`"models": {"worker": "worker.hdm"}`): glTF files turned into the engine's own `.hdm` by [`examples/remote/models/hdm.py`](../examples/remote/models/hdm.py), integers only:

- every triangle one color (its material's, or its palette texture's at its middle: low-poly packs paint with palettes), the corners lit by their own normals;
- a skinned model keeps its main bones (fingers and helpers go to their nearest kept parent) and its clips, sampled 30 times a second as each bone's turn (a quaternion of 2.14) and place relative to its parent;
- meters, y up, the model's front toward +z, standing on y 0, scaled to `--height`.

The engine reads it byte by byte (`model3d_load`), plays a clip (`model3d_sample`, between frames by a blended quaternion), chains the bones (`model3d_globals`) and skins the triangles on the processor every frame (`model3d_body_pose`: each vertex on up to four bones; `r3d_tri_pose` moves the body's triangles, drawn as an instance at the origin). A model without bones stays as built at `r3d_place` (furniture).

Furniture made by artists goes into the world before the light is baked (`model3d_add`: scaled per axis, turned, mirrored with a negative scale), so it gets the sun's and the sky's light like the rest. The demo's flat does it with `piece()` (room3d.c): the package's model fit into the box the built piece filled, its front turned to the side the built one looked out of, so the plan, the ways and the places of the game stay as they were; the flat is built again (about a second) when a package brings other models (`room3d_builds`, and the game builds its walk grid again). Two lessons: a solid of the whole box put the model's seat and cushions inside it, baked black, so a model gets a low solid a little inside its box (`r3d_solid_box`, 25 cm: walking goes round it, the floor under it darkens); and an L-shaped sofa does not fill its box, so it gets one solid per arm or the place in front of its seat cannot be reached (`tools/remotebots --content` walks to every place in the package's flat). A screen made by an artist is a dark box: the game's picture that glows (`pane`, `r3d_glow`) goes just in front of the model's screen, measured once from the model (the desk's monitors); in the same way a lamp's glowing bulb is built inside the model's shade. Small things on a desk (a mouse, a monitor, a lamp) take `NO_SOLID`: the desk's solid is enough. A package holds up to 32 models (`MODEL3D_MAX`).

People made by artists take the same `actor3d_pose` (`actor3d_place_model`): their own clips where they fit (Idle, Walk, Run, Wave) and the pose's angles turned onto their bones where none does (sitting, typing, holding, lying, playing the guitar). Each limb is first made to hang straight from its rest (an artist's arms are open in an A), then turned by the pose's angle, so the angles mean the same for both kinds of people. The bones are found by name (`Hips`, `Abdomen`, `Head`, `UpperArm.L`, `LowerArm.L`, `Wrist.L`, `UpperLeg.L`, `LowerLeg.L`, and `.R`). The demo's flat uses a package's `"worker"` and `"flatmate"` (`room3d_person_model`), else its built people. Two skinned people (about 5,800 triangles each) cost about 1 ms a frame.

### Walking (`src/3d/play3d.h`)

What a game played on foot needs, all integers:

| Call | What |
|---|---|
| `play3d_grid(lo, hi, floor_y)` | After the bake, a walk grid of 10 cm cells over lo..hi: a cell is free when there is floor under it (a solid just below `floor_y`) and nothing solid from 15 cm (a rug, a skirting board) to 170 cm (a pendant lamp) above it. The grid's box is where the game lets a person go: the demo's game stops it at the outer walls so the front door does not lead out. |
| `play3d_fits(x, z, radius)` | Whether a person of `radius` fits standing there (the middle and eight points around it). |
| `play3d_move(&x, &z, dx, dz, radius)` | A step that slides along walls, turns 30 and 60 degrees around corners, and steps sideways (up to 30 cm) into a doorway beside, so doors are easy to go through with a stick. |
| `play3d_stick(lx, ly, buttons, cam_yaw, &dx, &dz)` | The left stick (or the D-pad) as a walk relative to the camera: up is the camera's forward. The length (0..16384) is the tilt past a dead zone, so a stick tilted a little walks slowly. |
| `play3d_follow(target, ahead, yaw, pitch, want, &dist, &lift)` | A follow camera for worlds without a ceiling: when a wall stands between it and the person it first rises (`lift`, over the walls), and only then comes closer (`dist`). It rises before the wall comes between (it also looks at `ahead`, where the person will be in half a second), ignores what is within 30 cm of the person (a door frame) and looks at three points across the person, so walking along a wall never makes it jump. `dist` and `lift` belong in the game's state. |
| `play3d_eye(target, yaw, pitch, dist)` | The eye of that camera, for `r3d_view` (its yaw is the camera's, its pitch `pitch + lift`). |
| `play3d_route(slot, x, z, gx, gz, &dx, &dz)` | For people and pets the game moves itself: the direction from (x, z) toward a point four cells ahead on the shortest way to (gx, gz), around furniture and through doorways. Each of `PLAY3D_ROUTES` slots keeps the distances to its goal (a breadth-first flood of the cells a walker of `PLAY3D_ROUTE_RADIUS`, 23 cm, fits in) until the goal moves to another cell, so it costs a flood per new goal and nothing else. The distances depend only on the grid and the goal, so a save state needs nothing from here. Bots and tests walk with it too. |
| `play3d_angle(dx, dz)`, `play3d_turn(from, want, step)`, `play3d_sqrt(v)` | The direction of a vector (0 toward +z, 1024 toward +x), an angle turned the short way, an integer square root. |

A person's yaw for `actor3d_pose` faces the direction `d` when it is `d + 2048`; a camera of yaw `y` looks along `(sin y, cos y)` and its right is `(cos y, -sin y)`.

### The demo's flat (`src/3d/room3d.h`)

`room3d_scene()`, `room3d_light()`, `room3d_person(ROOM3D_WORKER or ROOM3D_FLATMATE)` and `room3d_cat()` give the flat, its light, its two people and its cat to whatever is played in it (the demo and the game). `ROOM3D_AT(cm)` turns a point of the plan, in centimeters, into the world.

## Performance

`make bench` turns the demo's camera around the house and measures a whole frame, on the 2019 Intel Mac Pro, with the engine's limit of 4 cores. These numbers are for the first flat (about 30,000 triangles built, 15,000 on screen, two people):

| Size | Average | Worst |
|---|---|---|
| 360p | 4.8 ms | 8.3 ms |
| 720p | 9.4 ms | 11.9 ms |
| 1080p | 17.1 ms | 19.9 ms |

(Measured with light maps and people, before the textures read between texels and the glass, on a quiet computer; with other work running the numbers go up by about a tenth.)

The quality pass of 2026-10-14 added these:

- round pieces lit smoothly;
- bevelled furniture;
- plants made of leaves (each a two-sided diamond lit by its own slant);
- door frames and open doors, skirting boards on every wall, curtains;
- posters and screens at 256 texels;
- bloom and vignette.

It brought the house to about 54,000 triangles. With other work running on the computer, a 720p frame is about 13.8 ms without bloom and vignette, and 15.8 ms with them. That is close to the 16.6 ms of a frame, and the next optimization pass will look at preparing triangles and filling pixels.

Before that pass, the 200 m² house that replaced the flat on 2026-10-13 was about 43,000 triangles. With other work running on the computer it measured:

| Size | Average |
|---|---|
| 360p | 6.1 ms |
| 720p | 11.2 ms |
| 1080p | 19.3 ms |

720p stays inside the frame. A frame at 60 frames a second has 16.6 ms, and the host also encodes the video, so **3D games run at 720p**; 1080p is a little over a frame. What costs most, in order: filling pixels (about 3 per screen pixel, nearest-first drawing keeps most of them cheap), preparing triangles, softening edges.

How to stay fast:

- Keep big flat faces as one box with `r3d_detail` cutting them, not as many boxes.
- Put things that are never seen (the bottom of furniture, the back of a shelf against a wall) out with `R3D_SKIP_*`.
- Build the world grouped by place (room by room): runs of 64 triangles then have small bounds and are skipped whole when out of view.
- Give the bake's grid only the area where shadows matter.

## Making a 3D game, step by step

This is the path the demo followed; another game follows the same steps.

1. **Plan the place on paper.** Write down its size in centimeters and where each room, door and window is (the demo's plan is at the top of `src/3d/room3d.c`: a 200 m² house of 1600 × 1250 cm). Give each room room to walk around its furniture. Decide the camera: the demo looks from above like a scale model, without the ceiling, so the walls are 2.65 m high and nothing covers the rooms.
2. **Write small helpers in centimeters.** `at(x, y, z, yaw)` sets the place, `bx(...)` adds a box, `cyl(...)` and `ball(...)` the round pieces, `mat(...)` the material. They keep the building code short and readable.
3. **Build the shell first:** floors (one textured box per room), walls (`wall()` in the demo: openings for doors, windows with frames, sills and glass that lets light in), skirting boards. Render it (`tools/hdrun LIB --demo 3d --shot 2 --out DIR --res 1080`) and look at it before adding anything else.
4. **Write the furniture as functions** (`desk()`, `sofa()`, `bookshelf()`, `plant_pot()`): each builds itself at the place set before calling it, with its front toward -z. Place it with the yaw that turns its front toward the room: 0 against a north wall, 2048 against a south one, 3072 against a west one, 1024 against an east one.
5. **Fill each room until it looks lived in:** the big pieces, then the secondary ones, then the small things (a mug, a remote, a rubber duck, cables, a parcel nobody opened). Ask of each room: does it look lived in, does it have a clear use, is it different from the others, is it interesting from several angles? Vary colors with a seed (the demo's `books()`).
6. **Add the lamps** (`r3d_light`) where the light really comes from, and make their bulbs and shades glow (`r3d_glow`).
7. **Bake** once with the sun, the sky and the ground's light, and the grid around the place.
8. **Build what moves after the bake** (it must not change the grid): each moving thing is its own mesh, drawn as an instance every frame, with a decal for its shadow.
9. **Keep the game in the state.** Everything that changes while playing lives in `hd_state`, made only of 32-bit integers (positions as 16.16 meters, angles as 0..4095). The world itself is built from code or from the package, never from the state, so a save state stays small and portable. A built-in 3D game keeps its own struct in the 2D level's coin bitmap (`HD_GAME3D(s, type)`, checked by `HD_GAME3D_FITS(type)`), so `hd_state` keeps its size and every 2D save state still loads; a loaded save state brings the game's fields back into range (its `_sanitize`, called by `save.c`).
10. **Make the place walkable and check it.** Build the walk grid after the bake (`play3d_grid`) and flood it from where the game starts: every room must be reached. The first flood of the demo's flat found a sink and its tiles across the bathroom's door and a dining table too close to the sofa; furniture placed for the picture needs a way around it (about 50 cm) once someone walks there.
11. **Test every place on foot.** A test walks from the start to every place the game uses (each activity, each spot of the pets and the people the game moves) with `play3d_route` and `play3d_move` at the player's size (the game's `remote_place` lists them). It found a parcel and an umbrella stand closing the way to the front door and a pouf between the sofa and the armchair, which a flood of the grid alone had missed (the flood passed through gaps the player's body does not).
12. **Let the game play itself.** A day without drawing runs in half a second. Bots that play like people (they walk the house along `play3d_route`, so the way there takes its time; they react after a delay; they rest when tired; they answer what the game asks of them) play many days of each style: `make remotebots` prints how often a careful, a casual, a lazy and a workaholic player survive, miss the goal or are fired, their scores and how high the danger got. Tune the numbers until the styles end where the design wants (TELETRABAJO's phase 6: the boss's suspicion barely moved, a careful player's peak was 29 of 1000, so working and answering calm less and being away weighs more; section 13 of its design has the before and after). Keep the rule tests' numbers computed from the tuning macros, so they survive the next tuning.
13. **Measure and look.** `make bench` for the time, `hdrun --shot` for the picture at 360p, 720p and 1080p, and the compatibility check below. Measure before and after a change on the same load (the machine's other work moves `make bench` by half): build the previous commit in a `git worktree` and run both one after the other.
14. **Things in the hands.** A pose's `prop` is a mesh held between both hands (`hold`) or in the right one, built around where it is gripped with its front toward -z, tilted by `prop_pitch` and `prop_roll` (a guitar's neck up). The game picks it by what is being done (`activity_prop` in TELETRABAJO: a mug, a pan, a controller, a guitar, a watering can, a parcel, headphones). Look at each one from the front and from the game's camera: a pan behind a counter is hidden, a guitar first came out with its neck down.
15. **Effects that move**, like water, are instances too: a mesh of see-through streaks (`r3d_glass`, `r3d_occlude(0)`) moved down a little every frame and wrapped (TELETRABAJO's shower: 28 cm streaks, moved 7 cm a frame). Put them where they are seen from the game's camera (the shower first fell between the worker and the wall, hidden), and inside what holds them.
16. **Sound from what changed.** At the end of each step compare the game's struct before and after (`remote_sound(s, &was, g)`): a message arrived, a timer started, a mode changed. Then play a one-shot (`hd_play_at`, placed by where the thing is) or keep a loop going (`hd_loop`, called on every frame with its volume, 0 to fade it out). The rules never wait for the sound and a replay sounds the same. The recordings come in the package (`"audio"`); list every source with its license in a JSON file and let a script cut, level, loop and pack them (`examples/remote/audio.json` and `build.py`; section 8 and 13 of TELETRABAJO's design tell how the sounds were found).
17. **Texts in three languages that fit.** Every text in a table of `[id][LANGS]`, the reference first; a test checks that each one exists in every language and fits the screen at the size it is drawn. The engine's font has capitals, digits and a few signs (a small x alone is the times sign; inside a word it is the letter).
18. **A video for each phase.** A small program steps the game with a script, writes 720p frames (PPM) to ffmpeg and the mixed sound to a raw file, then joins both (`ffmpeg ... -f s16le -ar 48000 -ac 2 -i sound.raw`); check the sound never clips, look at a few frames, and keep the file under 30 MB to send it.

## Checking compatibility

Every change to the 3D part is checked against a 2D game:

1. Before the change, record the frames and sound of a 2D game through the API at each size (3,000 frames with a fixed script, hashed with FNV-1a).
2. After the change, record them again: they must be the same.
3. `make test` and `make NO_3D=1 test` must both pass.

On 2026-10-13 ANTÍDOTO's hashes (360p and 720p, picture and sound) were the same before the 3D part and after each step.

## Limits and what is next

- What moves has no shadow of its own beyond the round decal, and does not cast one on the world.
- Lamps light big faces per corner (cells of 60 cm), so their pools of light are soft; the sun's shadows are in the light maps.
- Glass among glass is not sorted (two panes, one behind the other, may show in the wrong order).
- 1080p is a little over a frame's time: 720p is the size for 3D games.
- There is no 3D package format yet: 3D worlds and games are built in code (the first game is `src/3d/games/remote`, `docs/game-remote-work.md`). A package format, and Willy Maker's 3D editor, come later.
- The walk grid is flat: one floor height, no stairs or ramps.
