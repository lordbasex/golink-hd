# go-link HD in 3D

go-link HD draws 3D worlds as well as 2D ones: low-poly scenes rendered in software with integers only, so a 3D game keeps the engine's promises (the same pixels and sound on every computer and in WebAssembly, save states, no dependencies, no graphics card). This document is the reference for the 3D part (`src/3d/`) and a step-by-step guide to make a 3D game with it.

The first 3D scene is the proof of concept `golinkhd_load_demo(e, 2)` (`tools/hdrun LIB --demo 3d`): a lived-in flat on the fifth floor of a city block, seen from above without its ceiling like a scale model.

## Rules

- **One engine, 2D and 3D.** The 3D renderer is a separate part (`src/3d/`, its own header `r3d.h`). The 2D engine never calls it; only 3D games do. Sound, controllers, save states, packages, text and the API are shared.
- **Old games stay the same.** A 2D game is drawn exactly as before: the 3D code only runs when a 3D game is loaded. Every change to the 3D part is checked against ANTÍDOTO's frames and sound (see [Checking compatibility](#checking-compatibility)).
- **Integers only.** Positions, light and colors are integers or fixed point, never `float`, as in the rest of the engine (`src/fixed.h`). The textures and the baked light are made with integer noise and integer rays, so they are identical everywhere.
- **The same picture on any number of cores.** Drawing runs on up to 4 cores (`hd_rows`, `src/par.c`), but every core draws every triangle in the same order and the world is split and joined back in order, so the picture never depends on the core count.
- **It can be left out.** `make NO_3D=1` builds the engine without the 3D part (a smaller WebAssembly, an old Raspberry Pi); demo 2 is then the platformer.

## Units

| Thing | Unit |
|---|---|
| Positions, sizes | 16.16 meters: `FX_ONE` is one meter, `FX_FRAC(n, 100)` is n centimeters |
| Axes | x east, y up, z north; a yaw of 0 looks toward +z |
| Angles | the engine's: 4096 a turn (`hd_sin`, `hd_cos` give 16384 for 1.0) |
| Light, strengths, exposure | 256 is 1.0 |
| Colors | `0xRRGGBB`; textures `0xAARRGGBB` |

## How a frame is made

1. **Building, once.** The game adds pieces (boxes, cylinders, balls, quads) with a material and a place. Each becomes triangles in world space. Faces bigger than the detail cell (`r3d_detail`, 40 cm in the demo) are cut into cells so the baked light can show shadows on them; faces that look down are not cut. Texture coordinates follow the piece's own axes (a rug's pattern turns with the rug) or stretch a picture once over a face.
2. **Baking, once.** `r3d_bake` fills a grid of 5 cm cubes (voxels) with the solid pieces (glass and thin things can be left out with `r3d_occlude(0)`), then walks rays through that grid:
   - five toward the sun, slightly apart: a soft shadow edge;
   - up to 26 around the face for the sky: corners, the floor under a table and the inside of a shelf get less (ambient occlusion);
   - one toward each lamp within its reach.
   Big faces (floors, walls, table tops: at least 15 cm on each side, not looking down, not glowing) get a **light map**: a small picture of their own with a texel every 5 cm that keeps how much sun and how much sky arrive there, softened once (3 × 3) to take the rays' grain away. Their corners keep the rest: the sun's light without shadows, the sky without its corners and the lamps. Smaller faces keep everything in their corners. So shadows are as sharp as the grid, not as the triangles. The grid only covers the box given in `r3d_light_cfg` (`lo`, `hi`): the flat itself; the city around is lit without shadows. The demo's flat bakes in about a second, on 4 cores.
3. **Drawing, every frame** (`r3d_draw`):
   1. the sky (a gradient) and an empty depth buffer;
   2. the world in runs of 64 triangles: a run whose bounds are out of view is skipped whole; the others are drawn nearest first (what is hidden behind them then fails the depth test before it costs anything), split between 4 cores and joined back in order;
   3. each triangle turned toward the eye is clipped to the view (in front of the near plane, inside the screen's edges) in camera space and projected with 4 bits of subpixel precision;
   4. its planes are set up (1/z, light, texture), each triangle on its own, on 4 cores;
   5. it is filled row by row: where each row enters and leaves the triangle is worked out at once, then each pixel tests the depth buffer, reads the texture (between its four nearest texels when it is seen bigger than its texels, so close-ups are not blocky), reads the light map between its four nearest texels (sky × the corners' light + sun × the face's sun light), multiplies it by the color, and turns the result into the screen's color through a filmic curve (ACES-like, worked out in integers);
   6. glass is drawn after everything else, over it, without depth: what is behind shows through, tinted;
   7. the edges are softened (a pixel that differs a lot from its neighbours takes some of them).
4. **What moves** (a character, a cat, a door) is an instance: a mesh drawn at a position and a turn (a yaw, or a whole turn with `rot`: `r3d_rotation(yaw, pitch, roll)`), lit by the sun and the sky as it goes (without shadows), after the world. A soft round shadow under it is a decal (`r3d_decal`): a quad that darkens what is under it.
5. **People** (`src/3d/actor3d.h`): a body of 11 parts (hips, torso, head with a face, hair and glasses, upper arms, forearms with hands, thighs, shins with shoes), each a mesh with its joint at the origin, built once with a look (skin, hair, clothes, shoes, glasses colors). `actor3d_place` poses it every frame into instances by turning each part around its joint: standing, walking (legs and arms swinging against each other, a small bob), sitting, typing (hands moving), waving, the head turned and tilted. A positive pitch swings a limb's end forward, toward the face.

## The API (`src/3d/r3d.h`)

### Textures

| Call | What |
|---|---|
| `r3d_tex_add(wlog, hlog, px)` | A picture of 2^wlog × 2^hlog pixels kept by the caller; returns its id. It repeats across faces. |
| `r3d_tex_pixels(id)` | Its pixels, to change them while playing (a screen that shows code). |
| `tex3d_build()` | Makes the built-in textures (`TEX3D_*`) once and returns their ids. |
| `tex3d_poster(kind, seed)` | A picture for a poster or a frame: a sun over hills, stripes, a terminal, circles. |

The built-in textures: parquet, long boards, tiles, patterned cement tiles, wall tiles, plaster, fabric, knit, two rugs, grass, concrete, brick, a city facade, wood grain, marble, brushed metal, leaves, cork and a soft round shadow. Plaster, fabric, knit, leaves and metal are grey on purpose: the piece's color tints them.

### Building

| Call | What |
|---|---|
| `r3d_clear()` | Forgets every triangle, solid and lamp. |
| `r3d_place(x, y, z, yaw)` | Where the next pieces go. Pieces are given in the place's own axes. |
| `r3d_begin()`, `r3d_end()` | The triangles added in between are a mesh (`r3d_mesh`): the world, or something that moves. |
| `r3d_material(tex, texels_per_m)` | The next pieces' texture: laid `texels_per_m` texels a meter, or stretched once over each face with 0. -1 is none. |
| `r3d_glow(strength)` | The next pieces glow by themselves (screens, bulbs, glass); 0 is off. |
| `r3d_occlude(on)` | Whether the next pieces stop light. |
| `r3d_decal(on)` | The next pieces darken what is under them (shadows, stains); their texture's alpha is how much. |
| `r3d_glass(cover)` | The next pieces are see-through glass (1..255: how much they cover), drawn after everything else. Use it with `r3d_occlude(0)` so light goes through. |
| `r3d_detail(cell)` | Faces bigger than `cell` are cut into cells for the baked light; 0 never. |
| `r3d_box(x0, y0, z0, x1, y1, z1, color, flags)` | A box; `R3D_SKIP_*` leave faces out, `R3D_PANE` keeps only the faces toward -z and +z (a picture, a screen), `R3D_TOP_ONLY` only the top (a floor, a rug), `R3D_INSIDE` turns its faces inward. |
| `r3d_cylinder(x, y, z, r, r_top, h, sides, color)` | A cylinder standing on (x, y, z); `r_top` 0 is a cone. |
| `r3d_ball(x, y, z, rx, ry, rz, rings, color)` | A low-poly ball. |
| `r3d_quad(a, b, c, d, color)` | A flat quad, one side. |
| `r3d_light(x, y, z, radius, color, strength)` | A lamp for the bake. |
| `r3d_bake(&cfg)` | Lights everything built so far (`r3d_light_cfg`: the sun's direction, color, strength and softness; the sky's and the ground's light; how far a corner looks for walls; the grid's box). |

### Drawing

| Call | What |
|---|---|
| `r3d_draw(surface, &view, &scene, instances, count)` | The world and the instances seen from the view. `r3d_view`: the eye, yaw, pitch, field of view, near plane, the sky's two colors, exposure, `smooth` (softened edges) and the light for what moves. |
| `r3d_project(surface, &view, point, &x, &y)` | Where a point of the world lands on screen (a label over a lamp, a prompt over a door). |
| `r3d_triangles()`, `r3d_drawn()` | How many triangles were built, and how many the last frame drew. |
| `r3d_rotation(yaw, pitch, roll, m)`, `r3d_rot_mul`, `r3d_rot_apply` | Turns as 3 × 3 matrices (2.14), for instances that tilt (an arm, a door, a falling mug). |

### People (`src/3d/actor3d.h`)

| Call | What |
|---|---|
| `actor3d_build(&kit, &look, fabric_tex)` | Builds a person's parts once (after the bake). `kit.shadow` may hold a soft round decal drawn under the feet. |
| `actor3d_place(&kit, &pose, out)` | The pose's instances (up to `ACT_INSTANCES`) into `out`; returns how many. `actor3d_pose`: where the feet are, the floor for the shadow, the yaw (2048 faces +z), `walk` (0..16384) and `phase` (0..4095), `sit`, `type`, `wave`, `look` and `nod`. |

## Performance

`make bench` turns the demo's camera around the flat (about 30,000 triangles built, 15,000 on screen, two people) and measures a whole frame, on the 2019 Intel Mac Pro, with the engine's limit of 4 cores:

| Size | Average | Worst |
|---|---|---|
| 360p | 4.8 ms | 8.3 ms |
| 720p | 9.4 ms | 11.9 ms |
| 1080p | 17.1 ms | 19.9 ms |

(Measured with light maps and people, before the textures read between texels and the glass, on a quiet computer; with other work running the numbers go up by about a tenth.) A frame at 60 frames a second has 16.6 ms, and the host also encodes the video, so **3D games run at 720p**; 1080p is a little over a frame. What costs most, in order: filling pixels (about 3 per screen pixel, nearest-first drawing keeps most of them cheap), preparing triangles, softening edges.

How to stay fast:

- Keep big flat faces as one box with `r3d_detail` cutting them, not as many boxes.
- Put things that are never seen (the bottom of furniture, the back of a shelf against a wall) out with `R3D_SKIP_*`.
- Build the world grouped by place (room by room): runs of 64 triangles then have small bounds and are skipped whole when out of view.
- Give the bake's grid only the area where shadows matter.

## Making a 3D game, step by step

This is the path the demo followed; another game follows the same steps.

1. **Plan the place on paper.** Write down its size in centimeters and where each room, door and window is (the demo's plan is at the top of `src/3d/room3d.c`). Decide the camera: the demo looks from above like a scale model, without the ceiling, so the walls are 2.65 m high and nothing covers the rooms.
2. **Write small helpers in centimeters.** `at(x, y, z, yaw)` sets the place, `bx(...)` adds a box, `cyl(...)` and `ball(...)` the round pieces, `mat(...)` the material. They keep the building code short and readable.
3. **Build the shell first:** floors (one textured box per room), walls (`wall()` in the demo: openings for doors, windows with frames, sills and glass that lets light in), skirting boards. Render it (`tools/hdrun LIB --demo 3d --shot 2 --out DIR --res 1080`) and look at it before adding anything else.
4. **Write the furniture as functions** (`desk()`, `sofa()`, `bookshelf()`, `plant_pot()`): each builds itself at the place set before calling it, with its front toward -z. Place it with the yaw that turns its front toward the room: 0 against a north wall, 2048 against a south one, 3072 against a west one, 1024 against an east one.
5. **Fill each room until it looks lived in:** the big pieces, then the secondary ones, then the small things (a mug, a remote, a rubber duck, cables, a parcel nobody opened). Ask of each room: does it look lived in, does it have a clear use, is it different from the others, is it interesting from several angles? Vary colors with a seed (the demo's `books()`).
6. **Add the lamps** (`r3d_light`) where the light really comes from, and make their bulbs and shades glow (`r3d_glow`).
7. **Bake** once with the sun, the sky and the ground's light, and the grid around the place.
8. **Build what moves after the bake** (it must not change the grid): each moving thing is its own mesh, drawn as an instance every frame, with a decal for its shadow.
9. **Keep the game in the state.** Everything that changes while playing lives in `hd_state`, made only of 32-bit integers (positions as 16.16 meters, angles as 0..4095). The world itself is built from code or from the package, never from the state, so a save state stays small and portable.
10. **Measure and look.** `make bench` for the time, `hdrun --shot` for the picture at 360p, 720p and 1080p, and the compatibility check below.

## Checking compatibility

Every change to the 3D part is checked against a 2D game:

1. Before the change, record the frames and sound of a 2D game through the API at each size (3,000 frames with a fixed script, hashed with FNV-1a).
2. After the change, record them again: they must be the same.
3. `make test` and `make NO_3D=1 test` must both pass.

On 2026-10-13 ANTÍDOTO's hashes (360p and 720p, picture and sound) were the same before the 3D part and after each step.

## Limits and what is next

- What moves has no shadow of its own beyond the round decal, and does not cast one on the world.
- Lamps light big faces per corner (cells of 60 cm), so their pools of light are soft; the sun's shadows are in the light maps.
- Glass among glass is not sorted (two panes, one behind the other, may show in the wrong order).
- 1080p is a little over a frame's time: 720p is the size for 3D games.
- There is no 3D package format yet: 3D worlds are built in code. A package format, and Willy Maker's 3D editor, come later.
